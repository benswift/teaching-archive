## New Ideas

A new idea that I am considering so far is a bed which uses multiple sensors, AI, and maybe some basic robotics, which is explored deeper below.

### Roomba-esque Bed?

This was motivated by a portable robotic toilet I saw on the campus tour of BIT which involved the Robotics and Engineering Lab. The toilet could do a host of amazing things such as coming to the user when called and leaving after they're done and recognise commands via speech recognition. This would make life a lot easier for people with disabilities and other problems which make bathroom trips extremely hard.

If a simple thing like a toilet could be modified so it is connected to a network and is a 'thing' in Internet of Things, then there's no reason I shouldn't be able to do the same with a bed. There's no doubt beds are an integral part of everyone's life as most people spend atleast about 40% of their lives on a bed, however, there has barely been any improvement in the type of beds in the past few decades.

The features that could feasibly be implement might be
* The ability to change sheets automatically when told to do so via a voice command and might require some robotics to be able to physically do it.
* Ability to heat/cool the bed.
* Embedded power points in the bed as a large amount of people use their electronics on their bed.
* Sleep monitoring - can use motion sensor to detect movement, temperature sensor to detect body temperature during the sleep cycle and the data can be forwarded to an app which lets user monitor their sleeping habits.

## Developing previous ideas

I am also growing quite interested in exploring some ideas involving drones. Not sure how feasible this is, (I just came up with it while writing this blog post), but there might be some potential to integrate my animal detection system for cars with drones.

### Drone Kangaroo detection

We might be able to fit a drone with sensors, such as cameras, thermal sensors, motion sensors and ultrasonic/wifi waves emittor to detect kangaroos and then deploy the drones in areas with high occurances of animal-car collisons. One of the lectures mentioned how Wifi signals could be used to detect people's passwords and other sensitive information by sensing the disturbances in the waves, which I hope could be applied to detect kangaroos and other animals instead. The data could then be sent to navigation apps as the drone monitors the movement of the animals and displays it in real-time on the navigation app so the drivers are more cautious and aware of the dangers and have a better chance to avoid it.

However, the drones might not be entirely feasible as they'll need to be self-docking/self-charging and will need to be constantly flying around residential areas and will require flying permits.
