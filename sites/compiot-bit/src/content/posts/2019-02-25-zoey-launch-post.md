We have been updating on our blog posts every week about our project, Drum Transcriber and today is finally the releasing day for the source code!

The Drum Transcriber is an IoT device designed by William Cashman and Zhuo Zoey Chen, which captures and translates drum inputs into drum sheet music. In other words, it is able to interpret the drum hits graphically on website. It is created in order to help and inspire the music creators and connect all the people who are interested in music.

The source code can be accessed at [our GitHub repository](https://github.com/Wcashman1351/Drum_Transcriber_ASC/blob/master/Transcriber). The weekly update on the project is included in our blog posts: [Zoey's blog posts](https://cs.anu.edu.au/courses/china-study-tour/news/#zoey-chen) and [Will's blog posts](https://cs.anu.edu.au/courses/china-study-tour/news/#william-cashman), while Zoey's implementation is focused on the micro-controller and the sensor and Will's implementation is focused on the web server. The reports of our project, aka the design rationales are included in our blog posts. Click [Zoey's design rationale](https://cs.anu.edu.au/courses/china-study-tour/news/2019/02/18/zoey-design-rationale/) or [Will's design rationale](https://cs.anu.edu.au/courses/china-study-tour/news/2019/02/18/Drum-Transcriber-Will/) if you are too lazy to look for them from the blog post pages.

We attended an exhibition last Friday and did a quick 5 minutes demo to introduce our Drum Transcriber to audiences with varies background in computer science. Unfortunately we didn't take any pictures or videos that day, but we do have pictures and videos from our testing stage.
