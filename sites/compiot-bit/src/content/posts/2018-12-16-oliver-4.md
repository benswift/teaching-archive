# Useful Cases

At this stage David and I are trying to nail down an exact design of our system. We've considered three different use cases. Below is a high-level graphic of the system with all extensions (able to complete all use cases). We plan to use the ESP 32 board as it is relatively powerful, provides support for Bluetooth and Wifi connections, and at a very low price point. <LINK TO DAVID'S BLOG> has talked about the board briefly in his post and I plan to discuss the board a bit more in future posts. He also talks about why we're choosing Bluetooth over Wifi or Zigbee or other protocols.

## UC1: Distributed Voting

> "First assign a different characteristic of music to each glowstick. By wearing the Glowstick the user prefers this type of music. Characteristics may be artist, era, beats per minute, instrumental group but in the first iteration will be simply be genre. The user wears the glowstick when entering the club. AS they move around their presence in a specific room indicates a vote for a particular type of music to be played in that room. These votes are transmitted through the network, collected at a central control node. This node can then inform the DJ of the proportion of votes for a particular genre. If no DJ it can automatically select which songs to play."

## UC2: Personalised Streaming

> "Using the same Glowsticks from UC1, the user now also has a pair of wireless headphones. At any one time these headphones connect to a one of a number control nodes, switching as needed as the user moves through the club. These control nodes collect votes from local Glowsticks only, (lets say 10 metre radius). With these votes it can inform the DJ of the proportion of votes for a particular genre. If no DJ it can automatically select which songs to play. IT will then stream this audio directly to the user's headphones. This means within a small space many different people can enjoy different flavours of music without pollution from different speakers"

## UC3: Lighting Displays

> "The DJ or an algorithm can use the votes to create a light show using the Glowsticks. The show is made up of a series of timed lighting instructions. These instructions are sent over the network and would instruct the LEDs to change their intensities at different times. The DJ/selector could make use of different characteristics of each Glowstick to send different commands. e.g. for every odd minute pulse _RnB_ voting Glowsticks, every even minute pulse _Jazz_ voting Glowsticks"

## Base level requirements

Our plan is to build a system that will implement UC1, but good design methodologies promote extensibility and scalability. As such our network will be built try support the network requirements of all three use cases in later iterations, but focus primarily on the requirements of UC1. Note however some of the tools such as the lighting display simulator may not be implemented by the end of the assignment.

For example, UC2 demands a high and continuous bit rate. Spotify exclusive premium HD quality songs stream at a rate of 320 kbits/sec and this means using Bluetooth BR/EDR instead of the low energy version as LE is optimised for continuous streaming. If we restrict the network to only use Basic Rate/Enhanced Data Rate BT we are unable to build Broadcast or Mesh network topologies and instead must use the point-to-point protocol. This does assume that the voting and the streaming of music are on the same network however.

As mentioned earlier the ESP32 board natively supports both Bluetooth, [implementing the v4.2 BR/EDR and BLE specifications](https://www.espressif.com/sites/default/files/documentation/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf). Although not the most current specification it will still support all forms of network topologies. Opting to use the Low Energy radio as it consumes between 1% and 50% less than the BR/EDR radio gives us the choice of all three supported topologies: Point-to-Point (one-to-one), Broadcast(one-to-many) or [Mesh](https://www.bluetooth.com/bluetooth-technology/topology-options/le-mesh/case-mesh) (many-to-many) networks. This means that one will be able to rave long into the night without having to change the battery on the Glowsticks, keeping the user experience smooth and unbroken.

The Bluetooth Special Interest Group (SIG) is marketing the **Mesh** protocol as a disruptive technology, and it certainly looks that way. It can support a large number of devices (greater than 32000) on a single Wireless Sensor Network (WSN). Each WSN has industrial level security, is isolated from others, and a power-efficient message forwarding protocol. This makes it aptly suited for control, monitoring and automation systems. Or in our case it would allow for multiple large music events to be held in the same place. When all of its features are implemented it is a very robust network topology and if you'd like to read more visit the [bluetooth SIG website](https://www.bluetooth.com/bluetooth-technology/).

However it is tedious to implement location services in a Mesh network. The Mesh spec does support broadcast like network messages that do not require acknowledgement by other nodes. Instead the **Broadcast** topology nativley supports location services. It's typically used in public places and retail locations to suggest offers or Points of Interest to nearby pedestrians as they walk around. Each Glowstick could broadcast to control nodes in a simple master-slave like setup (but not a Bluetooth piconet).

The **Point-to-Point** protocol actually has a small piconets that allow for one master to seven slaves and each node may be in other piconets to form an adhoc network. However this seems a more messy solution compared to the above two protocols.

Both Mesh and Broadcast would service the requirements for UC1, however the Mesh network is more extensible, robust and secure. It also seems to be a rapidly growing technology. Personally I'd enjoy the challenge of trying to build the more complex network, especially if it is relevant to industry. I do recognise it will require more work and might be more than I can chew. But just like a good meal, its best shared with a partner.
