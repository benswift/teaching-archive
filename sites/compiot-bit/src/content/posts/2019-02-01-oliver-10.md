### A Design Change

As mentioned last week I have redesigned the how the controller will work. I've purchased a [Raspberry Pi 3B+](https://www.raspberrypi.org/products/raspberry-pi-3-model-b-plus/) from Jaycar. I've installed the NOOBS operating system following the [installation instruction guide](https://projects.raspberrypi.org/en/projects/raspberry-pi-setting-up). Tim helped me as we had to do it on his computer as mine lacks a microSD card reader. The RasberryPi organisation has gone to great lengths to make it as easy as possible to get started on their board. I installed Raspbian, a flavour of the debian linux distro that is lightweight enough to run on the less powerful Pi. Here's a short video of the startup running on the TV in my room. I tried to make it a .gif and embed but I wasn't happy with any of the conversion tools I used.

Raspbian provides the environment to write rich Javascript Applications, meaning it is perfect for the Spotify Web SDK, necessary to facilitate streaming to the speaker. One of the issues to be addressed this week is to finally write the software that will select which songs to play and then pump that out to the speaker via 3.5mm audio.

I also want to visualise the data that is being produced by the BLE Network and would like to find the time to create an application that can do this in real time. Hopefully it would look like a simpler version of the itunes visualizer. Have a throwback to 2008 when music was stored on your local device rather than streamed from the cloud:

This would also make for a really fun demo. If gallery-goers could see in real-time how they are generating data, it poses them the simple question of "What other data am I generating right now that I am unaware of?".

I've been taking a glance at a couple of the Pi Music Box, specifically how it is implemented in [this project](https://www.codeproject.com/Articles/838501/Raspberry-Pi-as-low-cost-audio-streaming-box). The core idea of this project is to stream music directly from Spotify or another listed service. It does not provide the functionality to create or generate playlists. What this means is that I will have to write some JavaScript that can do the song selection/playlist curation simultaneously to the songs being played on MusicBox.

The other issue with MusicBox is that it can be accessed by anyone on the local WiFi network by navigating to `http://musicbox.local`. If left as is and then installed in a night club, it would be quite possible for a reveller to seize control of the democratically chosen music. Subjecting all to a form of musical tyranny.

Instead I am considering installing Spotify onto the Pi, or writing my own Spotify using the [Web Playback SDK](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-playback-sdk/reference/#initializing-the-sdk).

### Self Reflection

With a week to go I keep asking myself if I could have managed my time better or worked more effectivley. Between visits to my family and seeing Djokovic play in the Australian Open Semi's the time not spent on this project probably is a bit big. But I'm not diassapointed in myself. At the start of the course I had no clue what IoT was. I'd heard it used as a buzzword in some of the entreprneurial circles I'm in but that was about it. I'd naievly asked Ben in Feb 2018 if his COMP2300 course was one on IoT, and although definetly in the same space, working with microcontrollers is only a part of this.

Take away the inclass time in Beijing spent learning about IoT technologies, the hours spent reading, researching and prototyping over the past ten week probably now reach well up past the 100 hour mark. In my head I'm making the fallacy of comparing my project to something out of Software Engineering, as can be seen in my earlier design timeline form around week 4. Really this project has been much more exploratry rather than results driven. I've been able to discuss (Dis)Connecting together from a product (hardware + software) perspective and a social perspective. I've been able to wander down tangents that don't make it to the weekly blogs but have left me with a greater understanding of how an IoT system works. I've been able to craft my own business ideas and investigate how cutting edge technologies such as BLE really work.

I've been able to learn at my own rate, something that I did not budget for. More learning at the ANU should be structured like this. Remove the lecturer, replace them with a convenor you can speak with for guidance. Let the learning be self-driven and therefore self-motivated.

This is one of the most rewarding projects I've completed and I haven't even finished it yet.
