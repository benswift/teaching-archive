### Progress

Adi and I have been meeting up almost every single this past week as we get closer to the deadline to finish the artefact. Earlier this week, we were able to send raw readings via bluetooth to our phones. We connected the microcontroller to an external power source (a power bank) and were able to get RAW readings wirelessly from other people into the phones. Although the data sent is still to the nRF connect app mentioned in earlier blog posts as the app development is taking longer than expected.

Also, since the alcohol sensor is typically meant to recive 5V of input power and the NODE-MCU32S only provides 3.3V, we hooked the ESP32 to an Arduino which in turn outputs 5V to the alcohol sensor.

![Boards connection](/images/posts/chinmay/Week11Connection.jpg)

On Wednesday night, we got some cheap Aldi wine and gave out measured cups of standard drinks at measured time intervals to people to correlate calculated BAC with the RAW readings displayed by the sensor. The sensor is highly sensitive but the readings always jumped when people breathed onto the sensor - which is what we wanted.

![Testing the sensor](/images/posts/chinmay/Week11SensorTest.jpg)

### Potential Applications

So as we get closer to the completion of the artefact and to the date of the exhibiton, it's important to address some of the potential ways PS Band will be used to connect people. Obviously, the intended purpose is to increase everyone's safety while not hindering any of the fun. 

Although, forseeably, the device has the potential to be misused by people to measure each other's BAC and turn it into a competetion - trying to reach the highest number of BAC on the device. One possilbe remedy is to cap the displayed BAC if it exceeds a certain threshload (such as > 0.08). This will discourage people from trying to get more drunk after reaching that limit.

There are also security issues associated with sharing your body's personal data with those around you - even if you trust them. I won't be going into too much depth over security issues but it *is* important to be aware of them.
