My previous blog posts have been highly technical — we've covered
microcontrollers, and memory management in Rust. We explored the distance
routing algorithm, and lamented over lengthy shipping times.

Let's get out of the weeds and take a birds-eye view. What does this project
represent? How does it relate to the theme, _(dis)connecting together?_

## Connecting Together

_Decentralised (mesh) routing. Distance-vector routing. Routing information
protocols._

These are all dry names for dry solutions to dry technical problems<sup>1</sup>. The
implications of these, however, are far-reaching.

Distance-vector routing is the foundational technology upon which the first LAN
routing protocol was built. This was repurposed for WANs, connecting routers on
the internet.

This (delightfully) simple algorithm paved the way for the modern the web.
Indirectly, distance-vector routing touched the hearts and minds of billions of
people around the globe.

Given my audience, I don't need to go into detail about the implications of the
internet. It transformed society in a infinite number of ways:

Better information accessibility & preservability (both educational, news, and
entertainment)

- Maintaining relationships — for all far-off family, friends & romantic partners
- Greater symmetry of information, arguably increasing the efficiency
- of markets Granting accessible anonymity to the masses (less true nowadays)

All in all, the internet connected the world.

And the general population has _no idea_ how the internet works. Some don't even
appreciate the web, instead disparaging it's effects on society and glamorising
the "good 'ol days".

Now, I don't expect my grandma to understand the ins-and-outs of old routing
protocols. That's not the goal of this project. This isn't a pedagogical display
for computer science students.

Our goal is to connect the layman and the practitioner. I want to create a
connection, however superficial, between the average person and this old (yet
incredibly important) technology.

If we get one person to say "wow, that's pretty cool" and go exploring on
Wikipedia, we'll have succeeded.

<sup>1</sup> Actually really interesting, but I'm not the target audience
