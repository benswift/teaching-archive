## Design Rationale

# What ideas or existing projects inspired your work?
This project was born from thinking about the world and the people around me in it. One day, on a bus, I realised that everyone around me was listening to music on individual headphones. I started thinking about whether there could be some way join these people together, allowing them to influence one another’s music. Initially I was only thinking about this in the context of being surrounded by strangers and aren’t interacting with them. But what would happen if I gave these people the chance to control what I was hearing? This would be an interesting social concept, forcing oneself to take note of others; their interests, tastes and very existence and allow it to influence a spall aspect my world.

# How does your project explore the theme?
The theme of “disconnecting together” is incorporated into our project by exploring what it means to “connect” and what it means to be together. The main way that our project relates to this theme is by creating a more social dynamic as compared to how music is currently played in group settings. It’s very common to simply have music playing from a speaker streaming from a phone, but the choice of what to be played next is ultimately being made by a single person, or increasingly by an algorithm influenced by a single person. Someone has a phone in their pocket which holds the key to selecting the music, but for everyone else in the group these choices are completely inaccessible. 
Back when CDs were the dominant way to store music, you had to store these CDs somewhere, making them accessible to everyone in your life. You could understand someone’s taste in music simply by looking at their CD collection. But now it’s possible to store tens of thousands of songs on a smartphone, or access tens of millions of songs on streaming services like Spotify. This sea of choice goes even deeper than this, as Spotify knows us all so well that it makes it almost too easy to listen to music you like. We listen to things on Spotify without the slightest idea of what song it is; all we know is the all-knowing Gods of Spotify thought I would like this song and they were right.
I think we as humans have lost some of the fun in music. It was once a social activity, but lately engaging with that social side of selecting and listening to music is something that’s becoming increasingly forgotten about. With this project I wanted to democratise music again and make what we hear a community choice that represents a particular group of people at a particular time. To combat the private nature of digital music storage, our project requires people to wear an object that prominently projects their choice to music to the world. Not only do I hope to publicise people’s music choices, but in doing so provide a catalyst for interactions between people who may not otherwise connect. By taking a part of one’s personality and making it so prominent, I hope to encourage people to talk to those around them - whether it’s because they have similar or different tastes in music. 
The way we hope to “disconnect” people is by designing the devices’ use to be incredibly natural and unintrusive. There is be no need for anyone to mess around with phones and speakers, to make or modify playlists or to faff about with the tedious process of getting devices to pair correctly. Users can disconnect from the technical mechanisms that provide them with their music, and instead focus on the music that is materialising, knowing that their preferences are being accounted for, and that the music that they are hearing represents a specific moment in time - certain people and the culmination of a series of choices and chance that is inherently unique and will never occur again. 

# What do you want the viewer/user to think/feel/do after they’ve experienced your artefact?
In some ways, I’d like users to think nothing after experiencing our artefact. My perfect vision of the system would make it almost completely non-intrusive; it wouldn’t feel like taking part in an influential communal voting system over wireless communication, but just letting the mood be taken over by good company and hanging out with great music, and the glowsticks acting as conversation starters, rather than the system and process itself being the focus of people’s attention. A more pragmatic goal however, would be for people to leave thinking “that was weird, but pretty neat”. I have known since starting this project that the final product was never going to be something that would change the world or make millions of dollars, but rather that the project would be something kind of fun and different, so if people are left thinking it was cool and thinking differently about the way they interact with music and others, I would consider that to be a success. 

# What design criteria did you choose to prioritise? which criteria did you have to sacrifice?
Unfortunately, despite my desires to have the device be simple and hide as much of the technology from the user as possible, this constraint has had to be lessened in order to make the project work in time. I spent quite some time working on making the system work with two types of devices: glowsticks which are worn by the user and allow them to select their favourite type of music; and speaker-hubs which would collect these results and make the actual music choices based on the data they get from the glowsticks. 
As part of this first design, the speakers would have had to act as “provisioners”, a term used in Bluetooth parlance to refer to devices which have the ability to add other devices to the network. Unfortunately, this has turned out to be a surprisingly complicated undertaking, and eventually we dropped this goal, now requiring users to add devices to the network manually using a smartphone app. 
Apart from this, the most noticeable changes to our design have been the stripping of features down to what is essentially the most fundamental iteration of the project. The reason for this is pretty simple: this project has been complicated enough already without us adding other things to it. 

# What was your design process? How did your project develop over time?
Our design process has essentially been to throw everything at the wall and see what sticks. Our actual design hasn’t changed much since our early posts, but the underlying methods to achieve that design have changed a bit, as we’ve explored options, either had success of failure, and then taken a step back to assess our position before moving forward with one approach. An example is with the speaker, in which we initially thought it would be possible to use the ESP32 to connect to the speakers, however it eventually was decided that we would use a Raspberry Pi for the speaker hub instead, as it has a built-in headphone jack and was easier from a programming side. 

# What were the challenges you faced in developing your ideas and prototype?
The use of the ESP-IDF was something we decided on quite early, thinking that it would give us more power and flexibility to work with the ESP32 boards compared to using something like Arduino. As it turns out, not long before we decided on using this, Espressif (the company behind the ESP framework) released a beta branch of their framework which supported BLE mesh, a low-energy mesh networking technology based on Bluetooth. We thought this seemed like great timing, and decided on using this for our project, understanding that it was beta software and might be a bit lacking in documentation. As it turns out, using this beta software has been very challenging, with very few examples, and the examples that do exist seemingly containing code that just doesn’t work. Additionally, without much documentation, it’s been hard to figure out the way things are meant to be done, so while code might work I’m never sure if there are easy or better ways that exist that I just haven’t discovered yet. 

# How has this project changed the way you think about IoT?
At the start of this project, I thought IoT was more-or-less a meaningless buzzword, but I now think that IoT is going to be an important part of the future of technology. I have been especially surprised with the available technologies for long-distance, low-energy wireless communication, with modules coming at a low-cost. During my time in China for our final presentation while at BIT, I did some research into the possible uses for IoT technologies in the financial services sector, which was an area that I thought had little chance of being impacted by IoT. But [a report I read from Deloitte](https://www2.deloitte.com/us/en/pages/financial-services/articles/the-derivative-effect-how-financial-services-can-make-iot-technology-pay-off.html#) made a compelling case that IoT will have impacts in the financial services sector, and I think that this is indicative of the many uses that this technology will have. However, I don’t think that the term IoT is necessarily going to survive as I think that the term has been too marred by security concerns and other issues that have surfaced in some IoT devices, but I do think that the future will involve more low-cost, connected sensors.
