# What I've Learned

## Wireless Networks and Communication

Computers become smaller, cheaper, and more powerful than ever. The same processing power that took the astronauts to the moon now lies in the palm of our hands. As a result, our computers are becoming more mobile than ever.

In the context of Internet of Things, the devices and sensors that are part of the system will almost always be mobile. The 'things' we want to 'internet-ify' (that is, connect to the internet) most likely will not be placed next to power socket and have a nearby Ethernet socket. For example, the smart water meter I proposed in my previous blogs will require it to be placed on the household's main pipeline, outside and most likely far away from the household's WiFi and power sockets. Thus, mobility is an important aspect to consider when developing IoT systems and devices.

Unlike personal computers that are stuck to desks, these mobile devices are not tethered to anything. How, then, do these mobile devices connect to services external to it? Wireless Communication.

Wireless communication has evolved rapidly in the past century and multiple forms of it exist today. From Satelite Networks to 4G, as developers we have a vast selection of wireless mediums with their own strengths and weaknesses. The most accessible and common is 4G, which will allow internet connection to any device with a SIM card.

## Data Science for IoT

Data Science is a field that seeks to obtain insight from data. As devices become evermore present in our lives, larger and larger amounts of data are being produced.

How is Data Science Relevant in IoT? Well, to 'internet-ify' a 'thing' involves adding an input sensor to the 'thing', which will return data about the activity being monitored. Data Science is essential for developers of IoT systems because it helps us to know:
.._ What kind of data we need to record and their corresponding sensors
.._ How to properly interpret the data receive
..\* What service or role our IoT device will offer the user with this data collected.

In the end, Data Science is used to help us humans make decisions. As a developer it is vital to understand it as IoT is highly reliant on Data. IoT developers are, in essence, Data Maestros. We collect data from places where it has never been recorded yet - we add our creative twist to the data, weaving and shaping it into our desired form, learning from it - and with it, we are able to offer a service or product that users can enjoy.

## Cloud Data Center for IoT

Cloud Data Centers are, like what it's name suggests, a Data Center in the cloud. By cloud, we mean the internet (but I hope you know that already). They are not only limited to Data Storage, but several Cloud Data Centers offer services too such as monitoring, search, online and data analytics services.

Cloud Data Centers are becoming an ever bigger staple in the world of technology. Almost every single major corporation uses a Cloud Data Center service.

In our context of developing IoT systems, Cloud Data Centers can be a major help, even a vital service, for our IoT systems. Cloud storage is mobile, it can be accessed anywhere and anytime as long as there is an internet connection. This serves us pretty well for our IoT devices which, as we have established above, are in essence mobile devices. These devices most likely cannot have 10TB of storage physically placed next to them. Thus, Cloud storage is essential for IoT devices, especially if the sensors on the IoT device outputs Big Data.

# Ideas

## Data Science-ifying Aspects of Life

I came out of the Data Science lecture thinking one thing: How did data all of sudden become such a big thing? The answer to that, I realised shortly after, was that Data Science as a field only emerged in prominence following the large amounts of data now available. Think about it; we carry around a hyper dense block of sensors and data called our smartphones everywhere we go, we have devices on streets and our homes recording our actions, from microphones on our Google Home to cameras on our streets. Data is being produced in a scale unseen in previous periods due to the number of sensors all around us at every moment of time in our lives.

So might as well add to it, right?

The question that arose to then was 'what aspects of us - our body, our life, our existence, do we not have a lot of data on?' Using an IoT device, we could perhaps gather more data and therefore more insight on these aspects of life.

I had a brief discussion with Kieran about this and we ran through some odd ideas, such as:
.._ Smart Bin that can collect Data about Rubbish Habits
.._ Smart Toilet that can collect data about bathroom Habits
..\* Smart AK-47 that can collect data about weapon usages

But so far, I like the idea of Data Science-ifying things through IoT. Stay tuned (or if I come up with something weirder than a Smart AK-47)

## Smart Luggage

Adding on to the whole Data Science-ifying boat - My Idea for a Smart Luggage may benefit from Data Science.

Outside of that scene in Toy Story 2, a lot of us don't really know what happens to our luggage once we let go of it when we check in. It just miraculously appears on a conveyer belt halfway across the world after your 12 hour flight. Coincidence?

Have you ever wondered how far your suitcase has travelled? Or how many times it was opened and closed? Well this can be answered by considering Data Science into our design for this smart luggage. Adding more sensors to the Luggage for the express purposes of Data Science may offer us more insights on both our travel habits and the airports and airlines we trust our luggage to.
