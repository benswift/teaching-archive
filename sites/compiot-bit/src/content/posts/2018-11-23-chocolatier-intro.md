This is chocolatier's course mandated blog for the Internet of Things China Study Tour. I will be posting roughly every week, as the [assessment requires](https://cs.anu.edu.au/courses/china-study-tour/deliverables/01-design-blog/).

Now, I view the Internet of Things as troubling at best, and downright disastrous at worst. Most IoT projects I have come across are either useless or convoluted solutions to simple problems (sidestepping the entire debate on the value of art) or provide mild conveinence at best (Home Automation Systems, Amazon Echo etc.). However, as [history](https://www.fda.gov/MedicalDevices/Safety/AlertsandNotices/ucm535843.htm) [has](https://www.wired.com/2015/07/hackers-remotely-kill-jeep-highway/) [repeatedly](https://motherboard.vice.com/en_us/article/aekj9j/internet-of-things-ransomware-smart-thermostat) [shown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirai_(malware)), these have massive cybersecurity risks. At the same time, these devices often have cameras, microphones, and a litany of other sensors while aiming to be as pervasive as possible. So in addition to the surveillance exposure to corporations and three letter agencies, we end up exposing ourselves to surveillance by hackers.

While I would prefer that IoT not be pervasive, reality sucks and it seems unavoidable. As such, I will explore the cybersecurity and privacy aspects of the internet of things. The ideas I'm primarily considering are

1. Automatic exploitation of IoT devices.

2. Low cost surveillance systems built using technology typically present in IoT devices.

While these projects are ethically dubious (to say the least), the hope is that by exposing these realities, people will be more careful about what they expose through these devices.

Speaking of surveillance, while far from the focus of this trip, The Great Firewall is simultaneously what I'm most excited and worried about. Poking at it from inside and figuring out how I can jump it (without exposing myself to commercial VPN providers) seems like it will be fun, but at the same time it *is* a massive censorship and surveillance mechanism I will have to live underneath.
