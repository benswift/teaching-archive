## Introduction
It’s time for your annual clean and check up with your family dentist. You walk in dreading every second of your time there. Finally it’s your turn. You walk in and see the dentist. The dentist looks back at you already judging your teeth even before he’s even seen them. Finally, he opens his mouth and the question you’ve been fearing comes out of his mouth. “Have you been brushing regularly?”

Imagine living in a world where you don’t need to have that conversation with your dentist. Imagine not having to be anxious about going to the dentist ever again. Imagine having amazing teeth for the rest of your life. All these things are possible through the use of my new smart toothbrush. 

## Inspiration
What is the best way to ensure that people maintain healthy oral hygiene? This was one question that I’d been contemplating for quite some time before I began this project. I started working as a dental assistant/receptionist early last year and came across many patients with poor oral hygiene. After seeing people suffering from severe dental issues due to bad brushing habits, I decided to come up with some sort of device that would ensure users to maintain a consistent brushing routine which would then prevent patients from experiencing any serious dental issues.

Humans are known to be lazy and careless. It’s who we are. But at the same time, our greatest concern is what other people think about us and we are willing to do anything to maintain our self-image. During my time in China, I noticed that public shaming was used to encourage people to follow certain regulations. Facial recognition surveillance systems were put in place to shame jaywalkers and then the photographs of the pedestrians along with their names and social identification numbers were displayed on LED screens on major intersections in Beijing. Debtors were also shamed in a similar way.

Putting these two ideas together, I realised that the best way to push people to have healthy oral hygiene would be to publicise this information so that people are then compelled to brush regularly due to fear of the dentist finding out. Furthermore, since the dentist has access to this data, he/he can give recommendations on how to improve the patients brushing.

## What is it/How it explores the theme
This smart toothbrush uses an accelerometer to collect the acceleration in the x, y and z directions. This data is sent to the board which is an ESP 32 microcontroller. I picked this board because it had wifi and bluetooth capabilities which was essential for my project. I’m then using a function to calculate the average acceleration and then using this to determine drastic fluctuations in acceleration. If there is a big change between consecutive average accelerations collected, then the user is brushing their teeth. I’m sending this information to a web server which the dentist also has access to. This project explores the theme of IoT through data that is being collected from a sensor and then sent and received with the use of the internet.

## User Experience
My goal with this device is to allow users to develop a systematic brushing routine and to feel good about themselves because theres nothing better than having clean, healthy teeth. If a good brushing habits are developed early on, patients won’t experience any severe dental problems later. I also want users to feel concerned about what the dentist will think about their brushing because this will drive users to change their brushing routines. Public shaming is beneficial as it can be used to motivate positive behaviour and responsibility. However, this device may also be considered slightly invasive since the mouth and a person’s dental routine is a very private matter and publicising it may cause some ethical concerns. Sharing private information can be quite concerning because it can be used in a negative way against an individual. Furthermore, public shaming can cause people to have a fear of criticism and social rejection along with effecting a person’s mental health due to anxiety. Thus, there are positives and negatives associated with the concept of public shaming and different people have different views on it.

## Design Process
As I was designing my artefact, my main criteria was that the toothbrush had to be user friendly and I had to prioritise comfort when brushing. My initial project was to create a toothbrush that would detect any irregularities within the mouth. But, after a lot of thought and extensive research I decided against the idea since it would require the sensor to enter the person’s mouth and that would not optimise comfort when brushing. The research that I had also indicated that my method of cavity detection wouldn’t work and what was recommended was beyond the scope of this project. Thus a design criteria that I had to sacrifice was being able to detect cavities. After a productive discussion with the store manager of the electronics shop I bought the hardware from, I chose an accelerometer to detect movement of the toothbrush which indicates when a user is brushing their teeth. This way I can attach the sensor to the bottom of the toothbrush and it won’t effect the users brushing. 

Another design criteria that I needed to prioritise was being able to send data somewhere that the dentist can also access. Initially, I thought of using two micro controllers and then sending the data from one board to the other where the user will have one board and the dentist will have the other. However, it wouldn’t really work in a real life setting since the boards would be too far to be able to transfer data from one to the other. I then started thinking of other methods and came across using the bluetooth feature on the ESP 32 after a discussion with a fellow classmate. My idea was to send the data to my mobile phone through bluetooth and then create an app which would receive the data. Unfortunately, as I started working on it I realised that given the time constraints I wouldn’t be able to finish the project. After further consideration, I realised that an app wouldn’t be a great way to display the information either because easy access for the dentist was another design priority and having the information on a computer would be more simple for the dentist to use. Therefore, I went with sending the data to the web server.

I faced a few challenges I was developing my prototype which were mostly to do with the fact that I wasn’t very knowledgable on the software aspects that were required for this project. I didn’t have much prior knowledge on how to use the ESP 32 and how to set it up so the first few weeks was mostly just doing research and building up the necessary skills to work on the software side of the project. This was also my first time being exposed to the C language which was a bit of a challenge as well. As I was developing the web server, I was required to know html and css and this was my first time using those languages.

## Similar Existing Works/Tying it back to IOT
There are many smart toothbrushes that currently already exist including Colgate Connect, Philips Sonicare, and Oral-B Genius. These toothbrushes are fitted with sensors which send the data to an app on the individual’s mobile phone and give feedback on how to improve brushing technique. These toothbrushes are impressive but what makes my artefact different from ones that are already existing is its ability to send the data collected from the toothbrush and send it to the web server which is also accessible by the dentist. Having an app that will give you live feedback is really handy but will users actually use the feedback? Probably not. My artefact guarantees that users will make an effort to change their brushing since it involves their brushing routine being made visible to the dentist and this why my smart toothbrush is different to all the other ones that already exist.

Before I started this summer course, I had only vaguely heard about Internet of Things. But over the last two months, I have learned that IOT is such a powerful concept and it can be used to do so much. As I worked on my artefact, I also realised that as IoT devices become more prominent in society it is also making private information more accessible to the public. This again ties into my main artefact idea of public shaming.

## Future Works
My artefact can be extended further in multiple ways. This project has a few shortcomings as it doesn't have any extra features. However, it has a lot of potential and once the features are added to the artefact it can be groundbreaking. The web server that I’ve created is quite basic and it can be updated to add more features and options to it. Another way the toothbrush can be made better is to add more sensors to it so other aspects of a person’s brushing can be measured. For example, data from sensors on the intensity of the brushing and how long the person spends brushing can be collected and sent to the web server. My artefact can also be improved by adding some of the features from existing smart toothbrushes and sending the information collected from the sensors to an app that is accessible by the user.
