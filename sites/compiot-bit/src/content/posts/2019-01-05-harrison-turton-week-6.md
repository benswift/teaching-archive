As end-of-year celebrations slowly cease, it's time to jump back in. After laying our roadmap, we enter the development phase of artefact construction.

**Artefact:** an interactive demonstration of decentralised routing protocols.

Bit of a mouthful, isn't it?

## Possible New Idea

In my previous blog post, I detailed the plans for our gallery piece. Multiple microcontrollers, paired with various buttons & switches, are to be mounted on a backboard. These can be controlled individually; through the buttons, or collectively; through the "base station".

I love the concept. It sounds mesmerising to watch a signal work through the network, illuminating an ever-changing web of connections.

But can the backboard-mounted microcontrollers do this justice? I'm not so sure. Though convenient, the backboard presents restrictions on our display. We risk reducing a compelling visual display to a set of blinking LEDs. Our work is our canvas, and our "canvas" is poky.

Instead of nodes on a board, can we place microcontrollers around the room, interlinked with long LED strips? This way, the room becomes the artefact. Rather than standing in front of a cute demonstration, we immerse the user in the network itself.

## Problems

There are two main problems with this:

* Long-ass, power-hungry LED strips
* Mounting on walls

Connecting nearby microcontrollers is easy. Connecting opposite corners of the room? That might be hard. Our Black Pill boards might not supply enough power for the longer lines. Perhaps we'll amplify our current with wall sockets, transistors and transformers.

Hopefully we can sidestep these issues. High voltages are scary, and I don't want to work with them. Maybe the LEDs can be powered with an extra set of AA batteries, or maybe there are transformers with integrated (and exposed) transistors.

Wall-mounting is a pseudo-social issue, not really a technical one. Though I want an enthralling artefact, I don't want to interfere with other artwork. Hopefully we can find a happy medium between our artefact and others.

Time to discuss this with my partner!
