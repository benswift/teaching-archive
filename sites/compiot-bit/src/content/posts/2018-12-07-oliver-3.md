# The Haggle

The art of the haggle. A form that I'm not accustomed to nor that comes naturally to me.

I spent a couple days this week trying to purchase Christmas presents (for myself and family). First at [Silk Street](WIKI), a tourist trap selling high quality knock offs of almost all FMCGs. Burberry scarfs, Gucci wallets, New Barren runners. They'll have it, and at a starting price suggesting a legitimate items. Although I'm reasonably sure that I paid one and a half times what I could get at [Alibaba](alibaba) it did give me some training before going to the proper electronic markets at \***\*\_\*\***Guancon.

From speakers to RAM cards, industrial printers to iPhone parts these markets were not as flashy as the tourist trap of the days previous. Instead the \***\*\_\_\*\***Guancon markets, as Tim (a tour member quipped): <CODEBLOCK> **It was as if a thousand computers had exploded and people had just set up shop next to it**. Unfortunately this place the merchants spoke no English instead pointing at calculators and using the little known fifth mandarin tone, Frustration, when had no clue what they were saying. I went there with the express purpose of finding a knockoff Arduino or other IoT device and unfortunately didn't come away with anything. However, I did spot a set of Wi-Fi enabled CCTV cameras.

It would be interesting to incorporate some sort of facial recognition into the system. Perhaps it could be used to add another dimension of demographic data to the music genre. Although there are dangers in generalizing one's music taste, as it is a very personal part of someone's identity. If some sort of ML was put behind the camera it could also be used to test the mood of a room, another dimension. However I don't know how pragmatic this is as DJs can just yell, "Is everyone having a good night?" and that usually does the job. Regardless, if there is a way to incorporate it into the system it would be dirt cheap to purchase it at Guancon.

## Bringing data out from the cold

I also visited two large giants of the enterprise solutions world. IBM gave us a crash course in big data and their IaaS business model, which fit in well with the week's lecture about big data science. It seems big data is a very expensive and low-density value industry. To increase value, it is best to have structured data that can be directly paired with other data points. This makes it easier to construe and analyse patterns in the data.

With this in mind, perhaps the music device could be extended to be a "TechnoClub" device that controls and informs patrons of other data from the night: what drinks they've had and when, specific gamified themes (e.g Traffic Light Party), how much they've spent, where they've been in the club. This information could then be accessed via a mobile application, or an API for 3rd party analysis. But as most students can relate to, when the night drags on its unlikely one's phone will make it. Moreover this hub of data and the use of the mobile phone detracts from the simplicity of a dedicated device for genre selection and voting.

Next we'll I'll upload a better plan of our device but here's a bit of a use case:

At the door of a club or party are boxes of glowstick like devices. A small tube covered in LEDs with a genre selector on the bottom. You put it around your neck and step inside. There are three rooms, with a DJ in each, everyone is wearing these glowsticks. You step into one of the rooms.

Meanwhile the DJ/selector gets feedback about the taste of the room and can suggest a distribution of different songs to play.

### Some other elements to consider with the system

- Silent Discos: The system could personalise playlists according to the distirbution of local genres
- Visualisation: DJs/Managers can see the distribution of music tastes in different part of the clubs
- Gamification: The glowsticks can be used as indicators for conversation or indicators to find like minded people.
