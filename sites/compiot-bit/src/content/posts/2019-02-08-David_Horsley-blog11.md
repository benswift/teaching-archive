## Riding the wave

Canberra gets a lot of flak, whether it’s about the perceived lack of nightlife (despite being home to both Mooseheads and cube) or the apparent lack of culture (despite our [“notorious penis owl”](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2018-11-16/phallic-owl-statue-erected-in-serbia/10504392)) but one great thing about Canberra is how close we are to nature. Drive two hours south and you’ll find yourself in one of Australia’s greatest alpine areas (whatever that’s worth), and drive for two hours east and you’ll have endless opportunities to see the pristine and extensive south coast. This week I took advantage of that proximity and spent the week at the coast with my family. It’s somewhat inconvenient timing given that we’re now only a stone’s throw away from the final deadline of our project, but I can thankfully say that this week has been relaxing, fun and productive.

If you’ve read any of my posts for the last few weeks, they’ll be no surprise that I spent this week working on BLE mesh, and unlike every other week when I’ve said I’m almost there, I know that this week I’m tantalisingly, tinglingly, finger-twitchingly close to getting this all working. The reason I say that is that having done more research, more trial and error and generally just digging deeper into BLE mesh, I’m starting to really understand some of the finer points of how it works on the ESP-IDF, and that allows me to say that we’re getting very close.
One of the bigger sources of progress this week has been looking at the [Zephyr Project](https://www.zephyrproject.org/), which is what the BLE mesh for ESP is based upon. It’s not always useful, there are large parts of the Zephyr BLE stack that don’t seem to have been ported over, but it helps in getting a general idea at times when the limited documentation from Espressif can be lacking. Apart from this, progress this week has also simply come from the slow process of trying to fix things by trying new things, inevitably breaking something and then fixing that, and repeating over and over again.

Earlier this week, I also had a very good meeting with my group-mate Oliver. At first we met to discuss our project, but after some discussion we decided it was time for a trip to Jaycar to pick up a few things to help us get moving on this project. Central to this was the purchase of a Raspberry Pi, which as of a few hours before this posting of this blog post, is successfully playing music from Spotify thanks largely to the efforts of Oliver.

One of the things I’ve done this week that was fun (but not related to uni) was a surfing lesson. Despite living in Australia for my entire life, and visiting the coast regularly throughout my life, I’ve never really been surfing. Aided a young German guy named Eric, who came here for a holiday and never left, I successfully managed to go from looking like a dork standing on a surf board on the sand to looking like a dork while standing on a surf board in the water. In the few days since then, I rented a board from a local surf shop and have been out surfing on two occasions. The first of these didn’t go very well: we spent a very long time getting the boards into the car and to the beach and when we got there, a combination of strong winds and ‘dumpy’ waves meant that after getting pummelled a few times and breaking a leg rope, we decided to pack it up after about 15 minutes in the water. Today things were much, much better. The waves were perfect, and I successfully managed to get standing up and actually surfing on most of the waves I caught. I don’t think I’m going to be dropping out of uni and living out of a van on the coast just yet, but it’s certainly been an awesome experience and something I’m looking forward to doing again.
On my first wave today, I went through the panicked thought processes that have accompanied all of my attempts at surfing for the last few days, a mental checklist of every bit of information about the technique and timing and little hints that I was given during the surfing lesson and things I’ve picked up since then. With all that going through my head, it took me a few seconds to realise that I was actually surfing. It seemed that after a few days of getting dumped by waves and sunburnt despite applying enough sunscreen to paint a small house that everything had fallen into place. And this, I hope, is what the coming week looks like for this project.

We’ve come quite a long way in the past few weeks. I’ve learnt a lot about mesh networks and hardware, and now it seems like all that needs to happen is for a couple of bugs to be weeded out and it’ll all work. Here’s hoping…
