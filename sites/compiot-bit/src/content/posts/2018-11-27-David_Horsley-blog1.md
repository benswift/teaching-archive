## Why China?

It’s a question I’ve answered many times in recent days and weeks as I’ve told family and friends about my trip to China. I don’t speak the language, I’m not studying the language and when I say I’m going to do a computing course, the response is usually “Why China?”.

It’s a good question, and in trying to answer it I’ve found myself having to take a step back and think about why I decided to go on a crazy adventure to China in the first place. The biggest factor for me was wanting to see China, and the (very generous) financial support alongside this program has made it possible for me to see China which is something I’ve wanted to do for many years. When I first enrolled at uni I picked a double degree with commerce because I couldn’t pick between the two subjects and the topic of China is one discussion where I find the economics to be fascinating. In 1990 China made about 3% of the world’s goods by value and now captures almost a quarter. The country has seen an unprecedented rise to become the 2nd largest economy in the world, and as a wannabe economist I’ve always wanted to see what It would be like to see China in person and experience the country that’s changing the way the world works. Computing and electronics are one of the best examples of the incredible influence China has had on the world; nearly half the world’s phones are made in China and almost every printed circuit board is made there, and so in many ways I think that China is the most logical place to go and learn about the Internet of Things, which is so heavy involved with hardware.

Despite being excited about visiting China, there are of course aspects that make me nervous. This trip will be my first trip overseas without family, my first time to Asia and the first time I’ve been to a non-English speaking country and I genuinely have no idea how much that’s going to impact the life on a day-to-day basis because I’ve never before had to think about language differences before.

But I’m also excited to see what it’s like to live somewhere where English isn’t the main language, and I think it’ll be interesting to be somewhere with so many people, especially compared with the often-quiet streets of Canberra. The actual uni work is also something that I’m looking forward to as I really enjoy making things, and over the years I’ve learnt CAD modelling, some woodwork and metalwork including learning to weld and I’m the proud owner of a 3D printer that one day I’m sure I’ll use to actually print something useful.

I’m a bit sceptical of the whole IoT thing and at times I think it’s a bit of a buzzword so I’m hoping that this trip can convince me otherwise. IoT is one of things that I think has huge potential, and I really want it to take off, but it seems every other day there’s a new bug, exploit or glitch ready to cause havoc ranging from spying on hotel rooms to mass blackouts. On the other hand, I came across an article on Project OWL, a project which aims to use robust networks of floating IoT devices to help save lives. These “clusterducks” will help first responders communicate with survivors, count the dead and plan evacuations in the wake of natural disasters. I’m hoping that while in China I can discover more of these kinds of IoT projects that have important implications and can really make a difference in the world.

## Ideas for the project:

I’ve only had a few ideas for possible projects:

- Using the diagnostics port (OBD2) to pull data from a car and use this data to bring useful information to the driver or owner of a car in a simplified way. While there are OBD2 dongles on the market, as far as I can tell most of these products are targeted towards petrol heads who want lots of specific metrics about their cars to monitor performance or for tuning their engines. I’m hoping my device would be more focused towards everyday people, with features such as reminding the owner when to get their car serviced while adjusting for how the car is actually used (e.g. more frequent services for cars which see a lot of stop-start driving, experience large temperature changes, etc). My main motivation is that my car (a 2003 Toyota Corolla) feels very “dumb” and as far as I can tell the data to make it smart is very accessible. It feels like this data Is currently just vanishing into thin air, and that if I could capture it there might be opportunities to do something useful, interesting or fun with it.

- Some kind of social music device, which can be connected to headphones or a speaker and changes the music being played based on the preferences of the people around it, which can be programmed into a wearable sensor or something similar. I can imagine this being a sort of fun novelty for parties or events such as silent discos, in which everyone could hear the same music playing but it is constantly being influenced by the people around, without anyone having to specifically go and request their favourite song or add it to a playlist. This idea is interesting to me because I like the idea of the world being influenced directly by the people around it, while also doing so in a very unobtrusive way. It feels like it would give everyone the power to modify the world around them (even if it is fairly minor way) without ever needing to lift a finger.

For the moment, I’m going to focus on surviving my first few days in China, starting to understand the strengths and weaknesses of IoT technology and assessing my current project ideas, while trying to keep thinking about possible project ideas as my understanding of IoT (hopefully) develops further.
