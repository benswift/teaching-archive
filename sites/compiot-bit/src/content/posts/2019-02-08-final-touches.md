## Final touches

... will be made when I get home from a week of holiday.

Not much progress has been made this week as I have been down the coast relaxing and putting off all my responsibilities. Even though no work has been done on the hardware or software components, I have had some time to think about the other things like how my water monitor fits into the theme "(dis)connecting together".

In my project plan, I introduced the idea I called "Home as a System", in which a place where someone lives has some intelligence to it, checks and data gathering processes happening in the the background to both warn someone of a problem, and give them the information they need to make informed decisions about things like water and electricity usage. The pertinent theme in that idea was that it required no interaction to function. It is something that can be completely invisible and takes no time out of your day perform its task. This kind of "built in" intelligence to a place was very appealing to me, as the idea of being informed without the need to be connected is a lifestyle choice rather than a requirement. People don't particularly need to be connected to all the systems in their lives, and it can become quite overwhelming if you try to do so. Just thinking about all the parts of your life that are back up by invisible systems is mind boggling, if you tried to maximise what you get out of every one of them you would be spending hours every day managing the way the influence your life. For example, electricity can be more expensive at certain times of the day, rewards cards can give you more points for buying certain products, petrol prices differ per servo etc. There are so many variables you may want to keep track of that take time out of your day to do. In some cases, the processes can be automated and monitored, and the ones surrounding homes fall under that Home as a system umbrella, where automation can save you money and make you more aware of environmental impact and other restrictions.

My point is that it is beneficial to manage theses parts of your life but doing so requires you to be connected closely to all of these systems. So in the design for my water monitoring device I wanted it to provide theses benefits with no interaction. I suppose not being connected doesn't mean you are disconnected, but taking the interaction-less design approach keeps users informed without the need for and effort. Disconnecting together, to be more present in other parts of our lives, while still having the same (or higher) level of information from the systems that support us, is what the design is all about.
