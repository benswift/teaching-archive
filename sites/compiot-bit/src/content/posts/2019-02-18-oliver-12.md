### Learning about the Internet of Things
My understanding of the Internet of Things was fundamentally changed by the time I returned to Australia on the 19th of December. Naively I had thought it was all about connecting your toaster or a door so that you could control it from your phone. Following the three weeks touring companies ans studying in China it became apparent that Big Data lies hand in hand with the Internet of Things. It has become very cheap to mass produce nodes in a network, add a sensor and two and one can generate Terabytes of data just by running these nodes. Lyft, for example, captures all the location data of their drivers, funnels it back to a data warehouse. They have shifted their business model to build AI Algorithms for use by other entities to manage their transportation needs. The Intelligent Glowstick is meant to give revellers a better shared experience at events. However it is also meant to serve as a reminder of how we implicitly interact with the internet of things and the algorithmic descisions of Big Data.

### The Experience of a Glowstick
As a reveller walks into the club they see a tub of brightly lit _Glowsticks_ of different colours. Each one labelled with a different genre: Rock, Pop, RnB, Jazz ... etc. They put one on around their neck and venture towards the dancefloor. The smoke in the air is lit up as people push through with Glowsticks around their necks. Each glowstick is one person's vote for a particular genre of music, a controller processing each vote and deciding what to play next. As our user moves through the room he can see people flicking the knob on their sticks to dark purple, and the song changes from Ariana Grande's latest ritzy pop to Lil Dicky's much bassier Professional Rapper.

This is the ideal use case.

I chose the glowstick as the tool to interact with the system because it simplifies interactions down to a non-tech object everyone can relate to. While the audience for the presentation will most likely be CS/Engineering fiends, the audience for the artefact is anyone who likes music (a much broader demographic).

Compared to the more interactive [Mash Machines](https://www.mashmachines.com/) DJ table, or the [Phonotonic control ball](https://www.phonotonic.net/) the Glowstick offers a less interactive experience. While people may come to adopt these more "hands-on" technologies in due time, I wanted the Glowstick to be a rather effortless step towards shared musical control. Rather than having to move block or throw a ball (opt-in interactivity), one is already getting involved simply by waring a Glowstick (and must physically opt-out of involvement). Guady as it may be, with everyone wearing the same device the Glowstick uses peer-pressure (Itself a form of group selection) to become normal.

Wearable/IoT company [Lucie Labs](http://www.lucielabs.com) have developed a wristband to be used at concerts that pulse with the music giving party-goers a more intense experience, while also sending data back to a central controller to determine how active a crowd is. The Glowstick acts on some of the same principles, but in a more active way. Lucie Labs still gives the performer the choice as to what to play, whereas the Glowstick gives that power, through proportional voting, to the reveller themselves.

### The User Experience
The intentions behind the glowstick are well developed, but Intentions, abstract as they, are exist in Plato's realm of Forms. To impart onto a user these intentions we can only control what they sense. We can only control their user experience. Consider Apple under Steve Jobs who made [$751,000,000 revenue](https://www.statista.com/statistics/263402/apples-iphone-revenue-since-3rd-quarter-2007/) in the first year of the iPhone, an exercise in prioritising user experience. 

Of course one can sidestep the trouble of User Experience, simply publishing a design rationale.

The Glowsticks are light enough and durable enough to be carried on a strap around the neck. This free up users hands so that they do not feel over encumbered by carrying the glowstick around. But it is not as convenient as putting it in your pocket. As it bounces off the users chest and the straps rub their collarbone it subtly reminds the user that they are currently generating and transmitting data. That their actions are being recorded.

### The Glowstick on Data Security
We do not recieve nudges or hints when other companies record our personal data. We are not told of every like we send on Facebook, of every site we visit and the advertising profile that is being built, or our location being tracked as we use maps on our phone. Take the following add, ING Online offering an interest rate 140% better if you give them 5 data points on your spending habits.

![advertisement](/images/posts/oliver-johnson/orange-one.png)

The Glowstick is designed to emulate the "Free" service model. That is; use our system and we will not charge you but we will take as much of your personal data as possible. I want to make users comfortable with using the Glowsticks, hence the focus on User Experience, but then gently remind them that the it is not as free as it seems. The Glowstick exploits your data to offer everyone a better experience at my gain.

### The Beauty and Challenges of the ESP32

We decided to build the glowsticks out of ESP32s and the network using Bluetooth Low Energy. The ESP32s were chosen for their onboard support of BLE, their low power consumption, low price point, their durability and the strong and supportive community. Neil Kolban alone having solved a lot of quirks of the board that I've had with development. But even with a large and active community, it did not stop me and David from over-estimating what we could accomplish and what underestimating how much time we would need. The consequences of our naivety(time and cost overruns) mitigated by adopting a strict timetable and an agile methodology. Our initial design included two extra use cases: one for personalized streaming, and another for co-ordinating lighting effects with the LEDs, that were not implemented in the final version. We had to remove this functionality due to the time constraints we faced. We decided to prioritise core functionality that gave users a "Plug & Play" experience, rather than extra added functions that may be underdeveloped and not tested as much. This strikes at the difficulty of my first project combining BLE, Microcontrollers and RESTful APIs. In the end with limited time we decided to prioritise form over function

### Music Preference

Our own musical preferences are rarely shared to other people. When asked to plug in the aux at a party consider how often will you revert to safer, more popular options, than to your own personal passion: [The 50 most essential Gregorian Chants](https://open.spotify.com/album/2lAxYEHQjJOf2cvB6vtit8?si=dK7kHH1PTNSnCAYT1piCtA). More often than not we will decide to self-censor our desires for fear of reprisal from the group. The Glowstick gives partygoers more control over the music they hear at their favourite club. By Connecting Together as the theme suggests, revellers are responsible for the music of the evening and therefore their own experiences.

It does this by offering users the opportunity to self-select into broader musical genres. These genres are unlikely to fully describe one's personal taste in music, making it realtivley riskless to publish a portion of their preference to other revellers.

Party-goers can see other people with the same coloured Glowsticks and flock to their kin. Or they may decide to throw themselves into an entirely different musical genre, its their choice. Either way it aims to remove this tabooed topic by gamifying musical preference and social interactions. [Liljeström et al in 2012](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0305735612440615) found that more intense emotions emerged in participants when they chose the music (as opposed to randomly selected) and when they were listening in a group rather than alone. The Glowstick aims to amplify this effect by giving users the opportunity to find people of like taste.

However the success of the Glowstick is heavily reliant on group dynamic. If people are unwilling or dismissive of it then it may not be normalized, and so the taboo of personal preference is not broken but instead re-enforced. Unfortunately this is an aspect of humans connecting together, when you bestow labels you are running the risk of creating divisions. Yet as the genres are not very descriptive nor relateable it unlikely to be a real source of dispute.

#### Algorithmic Control
[1.523 Billion users](https://www.statista.com/statistics/346167/facebook-global-dau/) log onto Facebook daily. To manage their experiences the company relies on algorithms. These algorithms decide what content we see, which friends pictures to view, which ads or which memes. They control how we _Connect Together_. 

The Glowstick does give some level of control to it's users but in the end it is the BLE snapshot and voting algorithms that will determine the outcome of the peoples vote. Without a physical DJ and without control over the playlist, users are gently reminded of their relience on Algorithms for their own entertainment. This is hoped to allude to other parts of our lives where we are subject to the folly of algorithms. More sinisterly it also alludes to Noam Chompsky's system to control a populace, Manufacturing Consent. If you control one's means of information you control their reality. No longer can they provide an informed descision. As we fumble our way through connecting online or through social medias we give up a lot of that control over information for an easier experience. We voluntarily submit ourselves to the algorithms.

### The Project
Working on this project has let me explore the vast topic that is the Internet of Things. Data science goes hand in hand with any sophisticated or commercialisable IoT device. With the rise of wearables we will start to see masses of human data rather than just environmental data generated. With this comes the greater risk for our own data to be exploited. I was astounded that your smart meter could reveal your daily habits to your energy company. I wish I had learned the importance of personal data security from school and not from having spent three weeks in a high-tech surveillance state.
