# Blog Post 2 – Refining ideas

My first blog post laid what I hope will be the foundation for my IoT project, by identifying a few possibilities for new or unique IoT devices for me to use in my project. Since the last post, I’ve thought some more about my two main ideas; a device to make non-smart cars smarter, and a smart speaker/music device which changes the music based on the preferences of people around. I’ve also come up with a few new ideas, partially inspired by the time I’ve had in Beijing so far. I’m hoping I can start locking down a (mostly) finalised idea for a project in the next week or so and can then start planning the device in greater detail. But for now, I think it’s important to try and asses my current list of possible projects and decide which direction to go in.

## Smart Pollution Monitor

Since arriving in Beijing, the pollution has been not as bad as I was anticipating, but is still something which has been on my mind for most of the trip, especially as heavy air pollution is not something I’ve experienced much before. Additionally, being unable to drink the tap water has been a new experience for me, and I’ve caught myself at the last minute with a mouthful of tap water on several occasions. It got me thinking about possible solutions, and I think a smart air mask or water bottle could be an interesting idea. It could collect information about levels of pollution in the air or water, the type of pollutant, the levels that are being removed by the filter, and the data from these sensors could be presented to users to let them know when they should take further measures to reduce their exposure such as staying indoors. Additionally, the data from many users could be aggregated and used to find areas with especially bad pollution, or if new pollutants spring up in certain areas. [Hardware to detect air pollution](https://altitude.tech/product/sensly-hat-gas-sensor-raspberry-pi-development-board/#configuration) does exist for IoT boards such as Raspberry Pi. However, these air sensors are quite expensive, starting around $100 for sensors that can detect particulate matter which is a major concern for some people in some areas.

## Smart Car Device

One of my earlier ideas was for a device which connects to “dumb” cars, and collects data about speed, usage, engine data and environmental data such as temperature and humidity, in the hope of combing this data and being able to gain value from it. I think remains viable from the perspective of hardware as [hardware exists for reading OBD2 ports with Arduinos](http://arduinodev.com/hardware/obd-kit/) and seems reasonably priced. However, as with in my previous blog post I’m still yet to find many rock-solid use cases for this technology, especially for consumers. The main value for this data seems like it would be for auto-makers, mechanics and other industry partners, who could access the data for predicting events or conditions which may precede higher demand for repair shops and other similar services, giving them valuable time to make preparations for spikes in demand for their services. However, the benefits for consumers don’t seem as profound, and it would be hard to sell consumers on giving up their data and not getting much in return, and I’m not really able to think of a large number of great uses I personally would get from this data. However, it is also possible that the data collected from these vehicles might be more valuable or might be able to be used in a way which is beneficial and just isn’t clear yet. One thing I have learnt during this week’s lectures has been that the uses for data aren’t always clear initially. One example was the lecture about privacy issues in the smart grid, in which data about the total power usage of a household can be used to identify what appliances are being used in a specific household, and hence can be used to track people and their activities within their homes, introducing significant privacy concerns that may not have been apparent when these power metres were first created. Just as it may not have been clear how this smart grid data would be used when these devices were first created, it may also not be clear what use (good or bad) the data from vehicles may have that I’m just not seeing yet.

## Smart Speaker

Upon thinking about it more and bouncing the idea off a few people I think one good idea is a smart speaker, which plays music based on people’s preferences and would be mostly used in social gatherings such as parties, rather than by an individual for headphones as I suggested in my earlier blog post. The speaker could incorporate other data such as noise levels in the room, weather or the type of event and modify the music choices based on this. For example, it could choose a playlist of “EDM focus” music for a study sesh where many people have identified an interest in electronic music. One concern with this idea is how to incorporate and identify people’s preferences in a reasonably painless way. One solution is to (somehow) gain access to people’s Spotify accounts, or other existing services with data about people’s music tastes. If this is possible it could be an elegant solution, however I don’t know how accessible this data is, and it would still require a way to physically keep track of people in order to decide who is still around, and hence will have their preferences impacting the music. Alternatively, I think a system in which people encode their preferences onto a tag, which is then carried on their person is a possible solution which doesn’t rely on other services such as Spotify. One option which I think would be fun would be to have a series of coloured pegs, with each colour corresponding to a genre of music. These can be pegged onto your clothing and have the dual benefit of allowing tracking of individual’s preferences (by the smart speaker) and as an icebreaker at parties by publishing your music taste to others in a fun and physical way.

Overall, I’m leaning more and more towards the smart speaker idea, as I think it strikes a good balance for affordability of hardware, usefulness and practicality, as well as being an interesting idea for a project and something which seems fun and interesting to design and develop.
