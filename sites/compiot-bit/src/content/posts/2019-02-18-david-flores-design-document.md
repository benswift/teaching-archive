From China to Australia a cultivating experience marking ripples through the currents of IoT. A network of devices that creates a connection/exchange without the intervention of a person, this is the basic definition of IoT. From this an idea was created and an artifact was born from that idea, this artifact is OfficEye.

## OfficEye:

Employers often struggle in keeping employees motivated and monitored to make sure they’re on task and resources aren’t being wasted. OfficeEye is the solution. OfficeEye will keep track of the time employees spend on their desks and whether they are working on their computers or not. OfficeEye will compile all this information and display it for users in a super easy to use UI for every type of user to manage all they’re employees, alongside a scoreboard visible to employees to keep them motivated and create a competitive environment encouraging employees to work more.

## Inspiration:
Growing up in an “office household” (both parents worked in offices), I quickly learnt of the problems and difficulties that modern day employers in office spaces encounter especially in the public sector. Problems varying from time inefficiency and wasted resources. These problems are and are not limited to: Employees wasting over an hour daily in everyday tasks like switching appliances on/off or adjusting appliances like thermostats, “long” lunch breaks and “long” toilet breaks as well as employers having a hard time monitoring all employees especially when small businesses are starting to grow into a more stable market.

These insights combined inspired me to focus my artifact on optimizing office spaces to facilitate employees and employers alike. This led me to the idea of the “smart” office, the idea of using IoT to connect various appliances and technologies and minimize time wasted to only one “switch”. Pursuing this I started examining office spaces and what technologies/appliances are used on a regular basis to get an idea on what devices I would have to work with. Once I had a rough idea of this, I held a meeting with my lecturers where I presented this idea to them. In this presentation, the lecturers gave me an insight and stirred me into a similar pathway on how to tackle the office space problem. That is to focus on the employee supervision and employee motivation over the technologies used as this would yield a better result on time and resources saved.

## Theme:
A disconnection is to be set apart from something else almost in direct opposition to the word ‘together’. Disconnecting Together however is something beautiful… the beauty in disconnecting together is being able to share a disconnection. There’s a sense of irony here because in disconnecting we are forming a connection “together”. This is exactly like my IoT artifact, disconnecting from the traditional uses of key cards and standard monitoring through human connection and replacing that with a digital connection together to the internet.

## Design and Implementation:
There were many routes my artifact could’ve worked in however I chose to prioritize on user experience and usability. In order to prioritize user experience and usability, I had to sacrifice technology “compatibility” and the artifact appearance as these are the features that will not be used by the user. In terms of technology “compatibility” I have used a variety of different programming languages and technologies for this project including, C, JavaScript and PHP.  Each corresponding to a different part of the project to create the best user experience possible. C and PHP were used for the Arduino Micro-Controller along with the Ethernet shield and Infrared Sensor to transmit the data to the firebase cloud database. On the other hand, JavaScript, specifically the React Framework, was used for the front-end/back-end components of the application. This combination of technologies are perhaps not the easiest, tidiest or ideal combination for the task, however combined they create an amazing and easy atmosphere for the user regardless of whatever background they have.

## Development:
The design process for this project begun with the creation of an artifact plan, in this plan a pitch was outlined, milestones set and a trello board detailing tasks that had to be completed were made here: https://trello.com/b/8vvpgd2T/officeye. What followed next was research in similar products/artifacts that have already been developed. 
This research proved successful as many artifacts with a similar goal were found, the most efficient/scary one was:

Humanyze - They uses badges that look like traditional employee identification badges but they’re much more robust. The company equipped them with radio frequency identification (RFID) and Bluetooth, an infrared detector capable of tracking face-to-face interactions, an accelerometer, and two microphones. The badges link up with beacons placed around the office to detect where an employee is at any one time. The microphones, on the other hand, creep frighteningly close to Big Brother territory but they don’t have the ability to record conversations. Instead, the mics measure tone, volume, and speed, along with potentially monitoring stress. The data processes in real time and delivers directly to managers in the form of an aggregated, anonymized view of their teams.

This is truly magnificent and frightening at the same time, and it’s taking my idea to a whole new level which I am not comfortable exploring due to the serious privacy breach involved. After this what followed was more research on how to capture the employee’s position in their desk. In the end I chose to use an infrared sensor along with an Adafruit Trinket M0 micro-controller. However, I later switched to an Arduino Uno Micro-Controller due to the incompatibility of the Adafruit Trinket with the WiFi Shield I needed to connect the Micro-Controller to the internet.

I then created a Firebase Cloud Database and started working on connecting the Firebase database to my front-end page which would display all the data from the Infrared Sensor. For this I used the React framework alongside node and ajax, and just tested it out on a localhost through my pc. I then continued by programming the Arduino Uno alongside the Infrared Sensor, and continued by connecting the Arduino UNO to the internet to send the infrared sensor data to my firebase cloud real-time database. I also used Microsoft Azure to host a PHP Web App that would send the Arduino Data to the database and display it as well.
I then continued by working on the front-end aspect of the web app making things appealing and responsive for the user and I followed up by finalizing the connection between the Arduino UNO and my Cloud Firebase Database. Lastly the only left was to start polishing the front-end aspect of the web app and to create the Gamification chart that will create incentive for employees to do more work.

## Challenges:
Throughout the duration of the course I faced several challenges varying from technical to non-techinal: Shipping delays on micro-controllers/shields being non-technical and for technical, problems like:

When my code compiled and was uploaded to my Arduino board, my Micro-Controller would then try to connect to the internet and then it would just kept saying “This site requires Javascript to work, please enable Javascript in your browser or use a browser with Javascript support”. After some research I learnt that javascript runs on the client (e.g. the browser) so when I send this request from the ESP, the server expected the ESP to run the javascript, which it can’t because it’s just a microprocessor so I got the error. The way I solved this was fairly simple, I decided to just go back to my previous idea of hosting it on Microsoft Azure because it doesn’t have that JavaScript security which free f2p hosting sites have in order to stop bots spamming.

Another technical problem which I encountered was that for some reason I couldn’t push remotely to the Azure File Directory, I was able to do it once and then afterwards it just kept saying everything is up to date even though that my local files were ahead by more than 2 commits. After a lot of research and testing, it turned out that this was happening because the current branch I was working on was not the master branch and the way Azure remote upload works is through the master branch. So all I did was move all the arduino-firebase files to the master branch in my github repo.

Lastly another challenge which I faced was that there was something faulty with my code that was supposed to upload the seconds of motion detected to the database. Instead of uploading that real-time, it was uploading it only once motion had ceased. This was probably the hardest problem I had and the one that took me the longest to fix, however after hours of grinding I managed to rewrite that segment of the code to make it send the data in realtime.
