## Artefact Ideation

Hello again, so I've been thinking about what to do as my artefact for this course for a while now and I have settled on an idea. It has been about three weeks since I first started exploring possibilities, talking to as many people as I could to generate and validate my ideas, and of course hearing some of theirs. It has been a long process of brain-storming and evaluation. Fleshing out an idea can be difficult, especially since I couldn't make it to China and learn about the world of IoT there.

This post will outline the idea that I spent the most time on, but eventually scrapped as it didn't come together as cohesively as I would have liked. Subsequent posts will continue noting my process and progress.

#### E-Ink displays
For a while now I have really liked the idea of e-ink displays.
![e paper display](/images/posts/sam/e-paper.jpg)
You may have seen them before, they are used primarily in ebook readers like the [Kindle](https://www.amazon.com.au/s/?ie=UTF8&keywords=kindle), but they have also been used in the Pebble Smart Watch ([RIP](https://www.theverge.com/2018/1/24/16928792/fitbit-smartwatch-pebble-end-support-date-june)), one of my favourite products.

I am interested in the displays for a few reasons:

- They are low energy, they hold their picture without power and only require energy to change what is on the display. This means you can update an e-ink display, then completely disconnect it from a power source at the picture will remain visible.
- They are reflective. In contrast to LCD screens, they do not rely on a backlight for the image to be visible. This means they are great for outdoor use, as the sunlight doesn't impact visibility.
- They have a great aesthetic. I'm not a huge fan of shiny things, I prefer a matte finish as it diffuses light and the appearance of finder prints is minimised. Most e-ink displays have a matte finish, and the idea of building them into some physical thing that is part of a larger display, is quite interesting to me.

Although the use cases for e-ink displays are few, I wanted to experiment with them which is likely why I spent quite a long time fixated on this idea of smart labels.

#### Smart Labels

'Smart Labels' as I'll call them, are small networked devices that can be mounted near items, to dynamically display information about them. My initial thought was to replace all of the price tags in a supermarket with small e-ink displays. I've heard from multiple people who have worked in retail, that replacing price tags or 'ticketing' is quite time consuming, and uses quite a lot of paper and ink as it is done regularly. My pitch for these smart labels in this context would have a few selling points:

- Reduce the paper and ink usage of the business, has both monetary and environmental incentive behind it.
- Cut down on work hours for ticketing, in a large supermarket, this can take hours of multiple employees time to do, and often bears inaccuracy.
- Allow for more dynamic information display, metadata about products and other graphics/styling can be changed easily.
- Updating specials and other prices can be done from offsite or automatically by the system, so that turn over time for a decision to change something is much shorter (minutes rather than days).

#### Why it fell flat

The idea didn't quite come together in my head and it felt a little impersonal. Supermarkets are an area of innovation I'm not particularly interested in, and this project seemed to be a bit more business strategy than engineering, which is *super* boring to me. The most interesting challenges in this project are about scaling. How do you network thousands of little screens in a small space? Meshing, routing and other networking infrastructure would be a really cool route to explore and it greatly interests me. However, given the budget and scope of work, it seems that the proof of concept would be a microcontroller with a screen attached to it that shows something, which isn't particularly interesting.

I held onto this idea for a while, partly because it was the only one I had, and also because the technology did interest me and I was trying to find a way to apply it in an interesting way. Unfortunately I failed to do that.

#### Where to next

After having a chat with both Kieran and Ben about this idea, I ultimately decided to drop it. Ben introduced me to the idea of taking the artistic or data-visualisation route with this. Which was an excellent idea, but again I failed to piece together a cohesive idea that I could execute. So, I kept thinking...
