# A Stick

> If we were to freeze time and walk around the inside of my local club _Mooseheads_ on Uni Thursdays we'd see a lot of people standing around looking cool.

How much does your music taste define who you are? We spend a lot of time consuming audio let it be the newest pop, a true crime podcast or a piece of classical. Personally, I don't mind a bit of Australian rap. Your music taste is uniquely your own. For many it is a very private affair that when subjected to public scrutiny because "You've got the most charge left," and, "My new iPhone doesn't have an aux cord." Will cause people to stick to popular songs that everyone in the room knows. But this is like electing a leader just to keep the other one out of office. Sure, the one you don't like can't make the rules, but the person representing you doesn't actually represent your views.

We've designed a device that will allow you to wear your taste literally around your neck. Called the **MighIoT Stick**, it is one node in a network of other sticks. Each person has a stick and each stick has an assigned taste. The system will aggregate the tastes and then use proportional representation to automatically build and curate a playlist.

Each **MighIoT Stick** has an assigned taste, which in the first iteration will simply be Genre, and a set of LEDs whose colours change depending on the user's taste.

This is the first step in breaking down the wall of taboo and fear and hurried intimacy that surrounds personal musical preferences. By making a conscious decision to present your music taste to the world the user opens themselves up in a safe and simple way to others. A simple scan of a room filled with MighIoT Sticks might tell you that the group in the centre are fans of RnB, while over to the right is a splattering of Rock and Rap, and there's the one guy with a deep hue of purple representing his love of Jazz. The Stick introduces a new way of connecting with other party-goers together

There also brings an interesting psychological discussion around choice, privacy and perception that comes with self-categorising in any situation. But the uniqueness of trying to summarize one's musical interests into one word is challenging. In the long run I'd like to remove the feature to make the MighIoT Stick more nuanced to model music tastes better.

The device also gives users more power over the songs in a room. Rather than ditching a club because the DJ wasn't reading the room correctly, a feedback button on the Stick will give the DJ or the Song Selector algorithm instant feedback. Connect directly to the music and let the MC know what you want and what the room wants together.

The **MighIoT Stick** brings new questions to the table: How far are we willing to project our music taste? What does it mean to have a group selected playlist? Will people connect better together with congruent music tastes?

We hope it will answer some of these and more.

But how are we going to achieve that...?

## The Plan

We will be developing a prototype of our system on Espressif's System on a Chip. David has already ordered a few ESP32-DevKitCs from eBay to arrive in late December/early January. They are an entry level dev board which has all the I/O pins exposed which should make it easy to plug in the [T1-3/4 (5mm) RGB LED with Diffused Lens](https://www.pololu.com/product/2558). The onboard SoC is the [ESP-WROOM-32](https://www.espressif.com/en/products/hardware/esp-wroom-32/overview). It also has an onboard Bluetooth LE and Wi-Fi Radio which can form part of a Mesh network. It is only recently that Espressif have released an [SDK for Mesh](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/feature/esp-ble-mesh-v0.5/examples/bluetooth/ble_mesh/ble_mesh_node/README.md) and it is still in beta. With this in mind we may have to use a Broadcast network if the current SDK does not support enough of the BTLE Mesh spec. We also have the option of implementing Espressif's own Mesh protocol over Wi-Fi, but we'd rather try implement BTLE Mesh. For development of the node we will be using PlatformIO environment to manage libraries and dependencies. It has native support for the ESP32 framework.

The control node will connect to a local application running on either a phone or computer. From here the controller will visualise the proportion of tastes and use BT distance estimation (RSSI), which David has talked about. The local application will store a local version of the data, as well as the songs already played. It will co-ordinate with Spotify services to get recommended songs via their [Web API](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/). Of interest is the Browse API which lets developers harness the Spotify AI. You can seed recommendations by artist, genre or tracks. I am unsure as of yet if this application will be written in Python or Java, or if it should be written in JavaScript so that it can be taken mobile. Regardless it will require some server-side interfacing with the Spotify API.

## The Timeline

We have broken the next eight weeks into five distinct phases with four intermediate milestones. We consider a sprint concluded if the code has been written, tested and refactored; and documentation has been written to explain the functionality of the sprint outcome and how to interact with it.

Working in a team with David Horsely, we plan to touch base twice a week. On Sunday night/Monday morning we'll have a short conversation followed up by a short blog post summarising the work done that week. While not compulsory it is there to keep us on track from day to day. Our major weekly meeting will be Thursday/Friday where we assess how we're going, set goals for the next week and make sure the previous week's goals have been finished.

While we will both be responsible for the system and the on-board development. My primary function will be to create the controller node and get it to interface with the network. David's will be to get the MighIoT Sticks operating as a node within the network. We will both be researching information about our respective responsibilities. After this initial process has been achieved, we will be using Pair Programming to ensure high code quality and that we both understand and can write about the project for our presentation.

There will be a degree of taking it as it comes but with this framework, and the agile characteristics below we hope to stick to the plan and timeline as much as possible

#### 24/11/18 to 1/1/19 - Final Research

This initial phase is the final setup for the project. The 1st is a soft deadline. Really, we will begin to implement basic functionality whenever the boards arrive. However, this is important time that will be used to setup the repo, setup [our project management software](https://monday.com/), search for tutorials and how-to, as well as other administrative tasks.

#### 2/1/18 to 14/1/19 - Implement Basic Functionality (Sprint 1)

By the end of this sprint we will have implemented:

- A rudimentary BT Mesh network that will include at least two Stick nodes to test their relay ability
- Simple blinking of the board LEDs with colours based on preferred genre
- A central co-ordinator that can receive and act on data from the network.
- Distance estimation using RSSI

The completion of this "Base Case" will be out first major milestone and is due before the conclusion of this Sprint.

#### 15/1/19 to 28/1/19 - Feedback and Advanced Messaging (Sprint 2)

By the end of this sprint we will have implemented:

- A Feedback button on the Stick that will contact the controller in real time
- Lighting cues that can be sent from the controller to Sticks.

This is a minor milestone which must be completed before the conclusion of this Sprint.

#### 29/1/19 to 7/2/19 - _Multiple Speakers and Controllers (Sprint 3 Optional)_

This is an optional sprint that serves as an extension should we have enough time. Or if we need more time to complete the outcomes of sprints one and two it is an extra week that can be used. This sprint will allow for the provision of multiple controllers and speakers, implying that different music can be played on the same system. E.g. if there were multiple rooms in the club, each room could have their own networks that the glowsticks could drift between.

#### 8/2/19 to 15/2/19 - Polishing Project (Conclusion)

From Saturday the 8th of February its "Tools Down" which is our second major milestone.

This final stage of the project gives us one week to do any final polishing of the project. It is this week that we can write our Design Rationale and finalise our project ready for presentation. This is an important week as it allows us a bit of lee way to tie up loose ends.
