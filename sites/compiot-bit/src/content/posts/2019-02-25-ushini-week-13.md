This last week was full of last minute tweaks. Sections of code were lost. Tears were shed. Lead fumes were inhaled. But we held it together (with an inordinate amount of duct tape) and just in time for the official launch. In releasing my source code on github and videos of my girthy devices in action, I'm pulling back the curtains on my prototype. There is a lot of room for improvement and it is far from being able to unite large audiences together, but it's a step-ball-change in the right direction and I'm happy with that.

There are still a few kinks which need ironing. One of them being the accelerometer data which affects the tempo. It was very difficult to get consistent effects by shaking the device from side to side. I think this is because the accelerometer actually measures normal force. This normal force will oscillate between a positive and negative value if you move the device from side to side. So there is a very slim chance that the device will send data to the server when the maximum normal for is being exerted. So I thought, maybe moving the device in a circle ad different speeds would better way to control the normal force. Doing so did improve the effects in the changes in tempo, but not significantly. 

The gyroscope data for controlling the volume worked much better. Since this sensor measure rate of angular velocity, rotating the device about one of its axis gave good results. By good, I mean changes in the speed of rotation of the device consistently increased/decreased the volume. I could also see that rotating two devices in opposite directions causes no change in the volume while rotating them in the same direction intensified the change in volume.

## Now what?

It wasn't until a few weeks ago that I developed a clear idea of what I want people to get out of this device. As much fun as I had flailing around with three bracelets strapped to my arm alone, I think this device could be a powerful tool for music interaction when strapped to the flailing arms of a large number of people. With just three devices, it was hard for me to get this idea across since one device on its own can make significant changes in volume and tempo without the involvement of other devices. If there were 50 devices, the changes made by a single device would be insignificant. I also found that to achieve the best results, the movements of the devices would have to be highly synchronised. You can see in the tempo demo video that I am having to hold both devices in one hand. 

I think the fact that the device allows people to work with and against each other, to move in and out of sync and have this reflected in the music (at least the volume) makes room for some interesting dynamics between audience members. Some people might lose friends in an effort to counteract changes in volume, and other might gain friends as they sway their arms together from left to right. I suppose this is what I want people to take away from this IoT device on a higher level. I want it to bring people together. I want it to encourage human-to-human interactions. In an age where people spend a lot of time interacting with interconnected things embedded with sensors for their convenience, it couldn't hurt to have a device which encourages people to interact with each other and with some sweet, sweet, molasses beats, could it?
