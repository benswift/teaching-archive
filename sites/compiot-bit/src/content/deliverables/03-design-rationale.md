## Outline {#outline}

- **Due date:** Mon 18 Feb (midnight AEDST)
- **Mark weighting:** 20 marks
- **Submission:** through the design blog ([full instructions below](#submission-process))

As well as building your [artefact](/deliverables/04-IoT-artefact/) you need to write a 1500 word design
rationale. This document will describe your process of inspiration, iteration
and implementation of the final artefact.

The ability to articulate your work to others is an extremely important and
going through the process of writing this document is a great way to clarify
your thinking too.

A design rationale is **not** an essay; the point is not to make an argument but
to elaborate on [your artefact](/deliverables/04-IoT-artefact/). This means that you should critically
examine your product in relation to existing work and current trends in IoT.

A design rationale is also **not** a "nitty-gritty technical detail" type of
document, so it shouldn't have heaps of code or *in-depth* information about how
your artefact works. It shouldn't contain *all* the information required for the
reader to duplicate the setup---that's what the README in the [GitHub
repo](/deliverables/02-public-github-repo/) is for.

Instead, the rationale is your chance to answer the question "why did you build
what you built" in response to the design task (from the [artefact
page](/deliverables/04-IoT-artefact/)):

> to design and **build** a prototype IoT artefact exploring the theme
> *(dis)connecting together*.

## Spec {#spec}

Your design rationale should be 1500 words (no penalty for up to 2000 words, but
a 50% penalty for this assessment item if you go over the 2000 word limit).

Here are some questions you need to answer:

- what ideas or existing projects inspired your work?
- how does your project explore [the theme](/deliverables/04-IoT-artefact/)?
- what do you want the viewer/user to think/feel/do after they've experienced
  your artefact?
- what design criteria did you choose to prioritise? which criteria did you have
  to sacrifice?
- what was your design process? how did your project develop over time?
- what were the challenges you faced in developing your ideas and prototype?
- how has this project changed the way you think about IoT?

The tone of the report doesn't have to be super-formal, you can continue to
write in the "technical blog post" style you've been using in your [design
blog](/deliverables/01-design-blog/). You should
write as though you're writing for an audience of your classmates (i.e.
generally knowledgeable about the big picture IoT stuff, but without any
specific knowledge of your project).

## Submission process

Write it as a [blog post](/deliverables/01-design-blog/) for now (but you don't need to [publish
it](/deliverables/01-design-blog/#can-i-work-on-a-draft-post-before-it-goes-live-on-the-blog)
until the end of the course). You must publish the final version [on the
blog](/deliverables/01-design-blog/#spec) by the
due date [listed above](#outline).

## Marking {#marking}

The design rationale is worth 20% of your total grade.

The task will be marked on:

- engagement with the questions outlined in the spec
- clear and consistent writing style
- critical appraisal of project's successes and shortcomings
- reflection on the project's contribution to the broader IoT context

## FAQ

### Can I re-use stuff from my blog?

Yes, within reason. In fact, if you've been engaging with the theme (as
required in the [spec](/deliverables/01-design-blog/#spec)) you should have stuff that you can use as "raw material" for your
design rationale.

However, it's ok if the blogs are a bit sprawling and all-over-the-place, but
the design rationale needs to be self-contained and make sense. So absolutely
mine your old blog entries for ideas, but if you just copy-paste a few of them
together then that won't make for a very good design rationale.

### Should I include source code in my design rationale?

No, that's what the [GitHub repo](/deliverables/02-public-github-repo/) is for, and also the work-in-progress
[blog posts](/deliverables/01-design-blog/).

The design rationale document is a chance to reflect on the questions [described
above](#spec).

### If I’m working with a partner, do we have to write a rationale each?

Yes, you need to write a separate rationale. You can include your own
reflections, and it's ok if you discuss ideas (obviously) but your rationale
must be all your own material.

One of the reasons for this is that there are rules around how much of a
course's total mark can be group work, and so this needs to be separate.

### Are references included in the word count?

No.

### What's with the 50% penalty for going over 2000 words?

Look, the spec says 1500, so 2000 is *way* over. There's no penalty up to that
point, so this is a way of being generous with the "wiggle room", but still
ensuring that we don't have to read any *War and Peace*-length rationales when
we're marking.

### What's the late penalty?

The standard CECS policy of 20% per day (or part thereof).
