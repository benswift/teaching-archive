## Outline

- **Due:** during your [week 4 regular lab session](#which-lab-session)
- **Mark weighting:** 1 mark (hurdle)
- **Policies:** you must attend **your** lab session---no exceptions (without a
  medical certificate); for other policies (e.g. academic integrity) see the
  [policies page](/policies/)

:::info
This is a **hurdle** assessment---if you fail it (or don't turn up) then
**you'll fail the course**.
:::

## Description

In your week 4 lab you'll have a short (around 5 minutes) oral exam with your
tutor to discuss the basic concepts covered in lectures and labs **up to the end
of week 3**.

You should:

1. attend your week 4 lab session as usual
2. during the lab, the tutor will have a discussion-style oral exam with every
   student in the lab

## Content {#hurdle-lab-content}

The questions you'll be asked in the oral exam could be about anything we've
covered in the lectures or labs up to the end of week 3:

- logic gates
- number representations (e.g. hex, binary, twos complement)
- instructions & encoding
- arithmetic & logic operations
- condition flags
- memory
- branching

## FAQ

### Wow, a hurdle---that's scary. Should I be worried?

This is a **hurdle assessment**: if you fail it, you'll fail the course.
However, it's only worth one mark, and as long as you understand the basics (you
don't have to understand all the extension stuff) then you'll be fine.

### What do I need to bring to the hurdle lab?

Just your student ID.

### What materials will I be provided with?

You'll be given a blank paper notepad and pen---your tutor might ask you to
write assembly instructions or draw a simple diagram to explain your answers to
the questions in the oral exam. Again, you don't need to bring this---we'll
provide all the materials.

### Will I have access to a computer for the oral exam?

No, it's just a pen-and-paper discussion.

### Can I bring the cheat sheet?

No---if you're just reading random instructions off the cheat sheet then that's
a pretty good sign that you don't understand the course material, and that you
should fail the exam. The questions you'll be asked will test the basic concepts
described above---it doesn't matter if you don't get the syntax 100% correct.

### During my exam, can I ask a clarifying question of the tutor?

Yes! If you don't understand the question, don't just pretend and make stuff
up---that's a sure-fire way to fail the exam. Instead, take a deep breath (it's
not a race, you can take your time) and ask the tutor to clarify what they mean
with the question.

### Will all students get the same exam questions?

No.

### Can I attend a different lab session to the one I'm enrolled in?

**No.** (Except if you're in a Monday lab---[see below](#monday-students).) You
must attend the lab session you're enrolled in on streams. If you turn up to a
different one, you'll be turned away (and if you've missed your lab time then
you'll fail the assessment item and the course).

### I'm in a Monday lab group and I missed my lab again because Canberra day, what should I do? {#monday-students}

If (and only if) you're in a Monday lab, you can attend a different lab session
(i.e. one on Tuesday--Friday). If you can [let me know in
advance](mailto:comp2300@anu.edu.au) which lab session you're going to attend
that would be really helpful, so that I can make sure we don't overload any of
the tutors in any particular lab.

Please be patient if your lab session is busier than usual---the public holiday
thing is nobody's fault, and we're working around it as best we can.

### What if I can't make my lab session in week 4? {#which-lab-session}

The usual policies apply---if you get in touch with the course convenor *ahead of time* and you
*have a good reason* then we can figure something out. And if you miss it for
medical reasons you'll need a medical certificate.

### Will I receive my mark during the hurdle lab? {#receiving-marks}

You won't receive your mark during the lab session, but you'll know within 1
week of the final lab session (so by the end of week 5).

### How will I find out my marks? {#seeing-your-marks}

Marks will be visible on [streams]().

### How should I interpret my results? {#interpreting-your-results}

Your mark (out of 100) for the hurdle lab will be either 0, 5, 10, 20, 50, 63, 75, 88, 100.

**Do not worry too much about the mark itself** (this in 1% of the total mark, and all final marks are rounded
at the end of all courses anyway).

Instead, you should consider your mark as feedback on how you're going---here's
a rough guide on how to interpret your result:

- **0-20**: you don't understand many of the basic concepts you'll need to pass
  this course (that's ok - you can always take the course next year)
- **50**: you passed, but it's clear that you're struggling to understand some
  of the concepts we've covered so far. It's not too late for you to do well in
  this course, but if you want to do that you need to start putting in the extra
  effort now.
- **75**: there may be a few subtleties and more advanced concepts that you're
  a bit confused about, but overall you've got a pretty good grasp of what's
  going on in this course---well done
- **100**: you're right on top of things---keep it up

### I'm not happy with my mark---what should I do? {#unhappy-with-mark}

There are no re-marks, you get what you get. As discussed
[above](#interpreting-your-results), the actual mark *really* won't matter in
the end. You should consider your mark "useful feedback" and

If, at the end of the course, you look back on all your results in the course
and feel that your mark in the hurdle exam is not a fair reflection of your
performance in this course, then you can apply for special consideration (as you
can with any ANU course).

### Can I get any more feedback on how I went? {#more-feedback}

There's no more official feedback, but you know which parts of the exam you
struggled with (because you were there!) and you absolutely can ask your tutor
to explain that stuff to you in your next lab session. You [**cannot** ask them
to change your mark](#unhappy-with-mark), though.

### Can my tutor give me a *hint* as to whether I passed?

No.

### What should I do when I'm not having the exam with my tutor?

There's no set lab content this week, and the tutors also won't be able to help
you out with any questions (since they'll be conducting the oral exams). So you
can go over any previous lab material that you didn't get a chance to finish
yet, or just practice your discoboard programming skills. You can still chat
with your lab mates, but please keep the noise to a respectful level for the
oral exams going on.

### Do I really fail the whole course if I fail this hurdle lab? {#last-chance}

Yes. Although you'll get one final oral exam with the course convenor first, just to make sure
that it's a fair result.

### Why do you have this hurdle lab? It's really harsh! {#why-have-a-hurdle-lab}

If you don't have the skills to pass this assessment item, then you'll really
struggle to pass the course. Having the week 4 hurdle exam is an attempt to be
"cruel to be kind", and by having this early on in the course you can avoid the
pain of failing the course (and the cost of the course if you withdraw before
census date).

Failing a course isn't the end of the world. By having this hurdle lab, it might
be a chance for you to drop out of this course gracefully and focus on doing
well in your other courses. You can always take COMP2300/6300 again in 2020.

### I failed the hurdle exam, is there *really* no way I can pass the course? {#supplementary-assessment}

According to the ANU rules, any student who fails a hurdle exam but otherwise
[satisfies the course requirements](/policies/#final-marks-and-grades) (e.g. scores more than 45/100 overall) is eligible
for a [supplementary exam](/policies/#supplementary-assessment).

So, if you do fail, then you can continue to participate in the course as normal
(submit the assignments, sit the exams, etc.) but the best you'll be able to
achieve is a PS grade.

### I failed the hurdle exam but I'm required to maintain a full course load, what should I do?

You need to talk to student services. You don't have to unenrol from the course,
although you [can't do better](#supplementary-assessment) than a PS. There may
be possibilities to get approved for a lighter study load this semester, but it
all depends on your specific circumstances, so make sure you talk to student
services.
